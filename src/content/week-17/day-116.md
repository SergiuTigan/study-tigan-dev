# Day 116 — Blog Post #1: The Transition Story

> *"The best career content isn't advice. It's a documented journey with receipts."*

**Date:** Thursday, 11 September 2025
**Hours:** 2h · Evening session
**Topic:** Writing & Publishing Your Transition Story
**Phase:** Faza 4 — Portfolio + Job Hunt · Week 17

---

## What You're Doing

Today you write and publish your first blog post: the story of your transition from Senior Angular/Frontend Engineer to AI Engineer. This post serves multiple purposes: it builds credibility, it's shareable content for LinkedIn, and it gives hiring managers context for your career move.

Publish on Dev.to or Hashnode (or both). These platforms have built-in audiences of developers. Your post will be discoverable.

## The Work

### 1. Outline (15 min)

```
Title: "From Senior Angular Engineer to AI Engineer: What I Built in 4 Months"

1. The Why (200 words)
   - What triggered the transition
   - Why AI, why now
   - Your background context

2. The Plan (200 words)
   - Structured 20-week program
   - Phase breakdown (Learn → Build → Ship → Hunt)
   - Why structured self-study over bootcamps/courses

3. What I Built (400 words)
   - Project 1: screenshot + what it does + what I learned
   - Project 2: screenshot + what it does + what I learned
   - Project 3: screenshot + what it does + what I learned

4. Key Insights (300 words)
   - What surprised me about AI engineering
   - What transferred from frontend experience
   - What was harder than expected
   - What was easier than expected

5. What's Next (100 words)
   - Looking for roles
   - What excites me about the field
   - How to connect
```

Target: 1000-1500 words. Long enough to be substantive, short enough to read in 5-7 minutes.

### 2. Write the Draft (60 min)

**Opening Hook — Don't start with "I decided to learn AI."** Start with something concrete:

> "Last month, I deployed an AI application that [does specific thing]. Six months ago, I didn't know what a vector embedding was. Here's what happened in between."

**Project Section — This Is the Core.** Each project gets:
- Screenshot (embed directly in the post)
- What it does in 2 sentences
- The most interesting technical challenge
- Live link

**Insights Section — Be Specific and Honest:**
- "RAG looks simple in tutorials. In production, chunk size alone cost me 3 hours of debugging."
- "My Angular background was surprisingly useful — component architecture translates directly to AI app UIs."
- "The hardest part wasn't the AI. It was designing prompts that work for users who don't think like developers."

### 3. Edit and Polish (30 min)

- Read it out loud. Cut anything that sounds boring.
- Every paragraph should earn its place. If it doesn't add value, delete it.
- Add subheadings for scanability.
- Check that all links work.
- Add a compelling cover image (Canva or your project screenshot).

### 4. Publish (15 min)

**On Dev.to:**
- Add relevant tags: `ai`, `career`, `webdev`, `typescript`
- Set a cover image
- Publish

**On LinkedIn (cross-post):**
- Write a short summary (3-4 paragraphs) with a link to the full post
- Add a project screenshot
- Use relevant hashtags: #AIEngineering #CareerTransition #LLMs

## Tips for Efficiency

- **Don't aim for perfection.** This is a blog post, not a thesis. Good enough and published beats perfect and in drafts.
- **Use your own voice.** Write like you're explaining to a friend, not writing a textbook.
- **Screenshots are mandatory.** A wall of text won't get read. Visual proof is compelling.
- **Include numbers.** "4 months", "3 projects", "450+ hours" — specificity builds credibility.

## Key Insight

This blog post will be your most-shared piece of content. Career transition stories resonate because everyone has thought about making a change. You did it. Write about it honestly, with evidence, and people will respond.
