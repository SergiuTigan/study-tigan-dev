## What You're Doing

Today you run 2-3 full mock interviews. This is where all your prep — system design, coding, AI questions, behavioral — comes together under time pressure. The goal is to simulate real interview conditions as closely as possible.

## The Work

### Option A: Practice with a Partner (Preferred)

**Find a mock interview partner:**
- A friend or former colleague
- Pramp.com (free peer mock interviews)
- Interviewing.io (if available)
- AI engineering communities (post a request)

**Mock Interview Format (45-60 min each):**

**Round 1: Behavioral + Pitch (15 min)**
- "Tell me about yourself" → your 90-second pitch
- 2-3 STAR questions
- "Why AI? Why this transition?"
- "Walk me through your most challenging project"

**Round 2: Technical — AI System Design (20 min)**
- "Design a [X] system" (partner picks from your Day 127 problems)
- Whiteboard or screen share your design
- Partner asks follow-up questions and challenges decisions

**Round 3: Technical — AI Questions + Coding (15 min)**
- 3-4 AI-specific questions from your Day 129 prep
- One quick coding problem (15 min max)

**Debrief (10 min)**
- What went well?
- Where did you stumble?
- What needs more practice?
- Rate yourself: behavioral (1-10), technical (1-10), communication (1-10)

### Option B: Solo Practice (If No Partner Available)

**Self-Mock Format:**

**Session 1 (45 min):**
1. Record yourself on Loom or phone
2. "Tell me about yourself" → pitch (timed)
3. Pull a random STAR question → answer it cold
4. Pull a system design problem → talk through it for 20 min
5. Answer 3 AI-specific questions out loud

**Session 2 (45 min):**
1. Different system design problem
2. Different STAR stories
3. Coding problem with timer
4. Practice "Do you have questions for us?" (always have 3-4 ready)

**Review your recording:**
- How did you sound? Confident? Rambling?
- Did you hit all the key points?
- Were there awkward pauses or filler words?
- Did you give specific examples with numbers?

### Questions to Always Have Ready

When they ask "Do you have questions for us?":

1. "What does the AI engineering workflow look like on your team? How do you go from idea to production?"
2. "What's the biggest technical challenge your AI team is facing right now?"
3. "How do you evaluate and iterate on AI features — do you have an eval framework?"
4. "What does success look like for this role in the first 90 days?"

Never say "No, I think you covered everything." Always have questions.

## After Each Mock

Document:
- [ ] What questions were asked
- [ ] Where you struggled
- [ ] What answers need improvement
- [ ] Rating: behavioral / technical / communication
- [ ] Top 3 things to improve for tomorrow

## Key Insight

The difference between a good interviewee and a great one is reps. Your first mock will feel awkward. Your third will feel natural. By your fifth, you'll be genuinely confident. The only way to get there is to practice under conditions that feel uncomfortable.
