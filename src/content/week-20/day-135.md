## What You're Doing

Day 2 of active interviewing. By now you've had at least one real interview (or are deep in scheduling). Today, apply yesterday's learnings and sharpen your approach.

## The Work

### Iterate Based on Yesterday

Review your debrief notes. Adjust:

**If your pitch felt too long:** Cut it to 60 seconds. Remove the least impactful sentence.

**If you stumbled on a technical question:** Research it now. Add the answer to your Interview Kit.

**If you couldn't think of a project example:** Map that question to one of your projects and prepare the connection.

**If you talked too much:** Practice pausing after answering. Ask "Would you like me to go deeper into any of that?"

**If you talked too little:** Add more detail to your STAR stories. Include specific technologies, numbers, and decisions.

### Interview Pipeline Management

Check your pipeline status:

```
Active conversations: [list companies in process]
Interviews scheduled: [dates and companies]
Awaiting response: [applied but no response]
Rejected: [document and move on]
```

**Pipeline health check:**
- Do you have 3+ active conversations? If not, increase application volume.
- Are any companies moving fast? Prioritize preparation for those.
- Any take-home assignments due? Calendar-block time to complete them.
- Any follow-ups needed? Send them today.

### Take-Home Assignments (if applicable)

Many AI companies give take-home projects instead of live coding. If you receive one:

**Approach:**
- Read the full requirements before starting
- Time-box it (don't spend 20h on a "4-hour" project)
- Build it like your portfolio projects: clean code, README, deployed if possible
- Include your reasoning in comments or a brief write-up
- Submit early rather than late

**Your advantage:** You've shipped 3 full projects. A take-home is the format where you shine.

### Continue the Routine

- Pre-interview research for tomorrow
- Post-interview debriefs for today
- Thank-you emails sent
- 2-3 new applications if pipeline needs filling

## Key Insight

The companies that respond fastest aren't always the best fit. And the companies that take longest aren't necessarily ghosting you. Manage your pipeline like a product backlog — prioritize, but keep everything moving.
