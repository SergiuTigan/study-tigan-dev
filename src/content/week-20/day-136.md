## What You're Doing

Mid-week. You've had multiple interview touchpoints by now. Today, shift from pure performance to strategic evaluation. You're not just trying to pass interviews — you're deciding which opportunities are right for you.

## The Work

### Evaluate Your Options

For each company in your pipeline, honestly assess:

```
Company: ___
Role: ___

Excitement level (1-10): ___
Technical fit (1-10): ___
Culture signals (1-10): ___
Growth opportunity (1-10): ___
Compensation likely in range? ___
Remote/location works? ___
Team I'd be joining: ___
Would I be proud to work here? ___
```

**Green flags from interviews:**
- They asked about your projects and were genuinely interested
- They talked about real technical challenges (not just buzzwords)
- The team seems collaborative and skilled
- They can articulate what success looks like in the role
- The AI work is core to the product, not an experiment

**Red flags from interviews:**
- They couldn't explain what the AI team actually does
- "We're exploring AI" with no concrete product
- The role is more data labeling/ops than engineering
- Disorganized interview process (bad sign for company culture)
- No one on the team has AI engineering experience (you'd be alone)

### Later-Stage Interview Prep

If you're advancing to later rounds, prepare for deeper dives:

**Architecture review:** They may ask you to walk through one of your projects in extreme detail. Open your code beforehand. Be ready to explain every design decision.

**Live coding with AI twist:** Some companies have you build a small AI feature live. Practice: call an LLM API, process the response, display it — all under time pressure.

**Meet-the-team rounds:** Be prepared for culture and collaboration questions. "How do you handle disagreements about technical approaches?" "How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?"

## Key Insight

The best offer isn't always the first one or the highest-paying one. It's the one where you'll grow the most as an AI engineer, work with people you respect, and build products you care about. Use the interview process to evaluate companies, not just sell yourself.
