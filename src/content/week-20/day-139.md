# Day 139 — Negotiate Offers

> *"The difference between accepting the first number and negotiating is often $15-30K. That's one conversation."*

**Date:** Saturday, 4 October 2025
**Hours:** 3h · Focused session
**Topic:** Offer Evaluation & Negotiation
**Phase:** Faza 4 — Portfolio + Job Hunt · Week 20

## What You're Doing

Today you evaluate any offers on the table and prepare to negotiate. Even if you don't have an offer yet, study this — it's the most financially impactful skill of the entire 20-week program. A 10-minute negotiation can be worth $20K+/year.

## The Work

### 1. Know Your Numbers (30 min)

**Research salary ranges:**
- levels.fyi — most reliable for tech compensation
- Glassdoor — good for company-specific ranges
- Blind — anonymous real data from employees
- Payscale/Salary.com — supplementary data

**AI Engineer salary ranges (2025, US market):**

| Level | Base Salary | Total Comp |
|-------|------------|------------|
| Junior/Entry AI Engineer | $100-130K | $110-150K |
| Mid-Level AI Engineer | $130-160K | $150-200K |
| Senior AI Engineer | $160-200K | $200-280K |
| Staff AI Engineer | $200-250K | $280-400K |

**Your realistic range:** $120-180K base (mid-level, career transitioner with senior engineering experience)

Adjust for:
- Location (SF/NYC +20-30%, remote roles may pay less)
- Company stage (early startup may offer more equity, lower base)
- Your specific experience overlap with the role

**Set your numbers:**
- **Floor:** The minimum total comp you'll accept: $___
- **Target:** What you want: $___
- **Stretch:** What would make you ecstatic: $___

### 2. Evaluate Each Offer Holistically (30 min)

Salary is one number. Evaluate the full picture:

```
Offer from: ___

Base salary: $___
Equity/RSUs: $___  (value honestly — startup equity may be worth $0)
Signing bonus: $___
Annual bonus target: ____%
Total Year 1 comp: $___

Benefits:
- Health insurance quality: ___
- PTO days: ___
- 401k match: ___
- Remote policy: ___
- Learning budget: ___
- Equipment budget: ___

Growth:
- Will I learn? ___
- Will I ship meaningful AI products? ___
- Will I work with people better than me? ___
- What does the promotion path look like? ___
- Is the company growing? ___

Gut feeling (1-10): ___
```

### 3. Negotiation Strategy (60 min)

**The Golden Rules:**

1. **Never give your number first.** If they ask your expectations: "I'd love to understand the full compensation range for this role before discussing numbers. What's the range you've budgeted?"

2. **Always negotiate.** Even if the first offer is good. Companies expect it. Not negotiating leaves money on the table and can signal low confidence.

3. **Be enthusiastic AND firm.** "I'm really excited about this role and [Company]. I'd love to make this work. Based on my research and experience, I was hoping for something closer to $[target]."

4. **Negotiate base salary first, then everything else.** Base salary compounds — it affects raises, bonuses, and future offers.

5. **Get everything in writing.** Verbal offers aren't offers. Ask for the offer letter before making decisions.

**Negotiation Script:**

```
"Thank you so much for this offer — I'm genuinely excited about
joining [Company] and contributing to [specific project/team].

I've done some research on market rates for AI engineers with
my experience level, and I was hoping we could discuss the base
salary. Based on what I'm seeing in the market and the value I'd
bring — including my production AI project experience and senior
engineering background — I was thinking a base closer to $[target]
would be more in line.

Is there flexibility there?"
```

**If they can't move on base:**
- Ask about signing bonus
- Ask about equity refresh
- Ask about performance bonus
- Ask about earlier review cycle (6 months instead of 12)
- Ask about remote flexibility, PTO, or learning budget

**If you have multiple offers:**
- Use them as leverage (honestly, not manipulatively)
- "I have another offer at $X. I'd prefer to join [your company], but the compensation gap is significant. Can we close that gap?"
- Give companies a reasonable deadline: "I'd like to make my decision by [date]"

### 4. Practice the Conversation (60 min)

Role-play the negotiation:
- Say your negotiation script out loud 5 times
- Practice handling "That's the best we can do" (respond: "I understand. What about [alternative]?")
- Practice handling "We need to check with the team" (respond: "Of course. When can I expect to hear back?")
- Practice the uncomfortable silence after you state your number (say nothing — let them respond)

## Key Insight

Negotiation isn't confrontational. It's collaborative problem-solving. You're both trying to find a number that works. The company has already decided they want you — that's why they made an offer. You have leverage. Use it respectfully but confidently.

The biggest negotiation mistake isn't asking for too much. It's not asking at all.
